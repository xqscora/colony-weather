MODEL NOTES

What this sandbox does (and does not)

Each dot is a simplified yeast-cell agent sharing a local tile of a small culture dish. Cells consume local glucose, use an oxygen-dependent energy term, change mass, and bud into nearby space. Neighboring tiles exchange resources. Every step updates glucose, oxygen, ethanol-proxy and CO₂-proxy fields.

The fermentation share rises with high glucose and low oxygen, reflecting qualitative features of Saccharomyces cerevisiae metabolism. Coefficients are illustrative and dimensionless; they are not fitted to a particular strain or laboratory dataset.

Use it to explore model behavior, not to predict a real culture. It omits gene regulation, cell-cycle phases, strain differences, pH buffering, vessel geometry and many other mechanisms. A visually plausible result is not experimental evidence.

Background: Saldanha et al., “The effect of glucose and oxygen limitation on the physiology of yeast,” Microbiology (2004), open paper; van Dijken et al., yeast growth on glucose/ethanol mixtures (1982), PubMed record.
